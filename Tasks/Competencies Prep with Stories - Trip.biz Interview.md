# Foundation: stage, priority, role, competencies

The chain is deliberate. Each step is an assumption the next one rests on. If Jim contradicts step 1 in the room, steps 2 to 4 change with it, so hold them as a read rather than a verdict.

## 1. Which stage of business development Trip.Biz EMEA is at

**Answer: Stage 2 of 4. Post-acquisition integration and proof. Not entry, not scale.**

A four-rung ladder for a regional business unit, and where the evidence puts them:

| Stage                        | What it looks like                                                                                                                        | Trip.Biz EMEA                                                              |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0. Entry                     | Rep offices, no local delivery, no licences                                                                                               | Passed. London, Amsterdam, Edinburgh existed pre-2025 but delivered little |
| 1. Beachhead bought          | Buy a local company outright and inherit its client book, staff, licences and country footprint, instead of winning clients one at a time | **Done 15 Sep 2025** (Key Travel EMEA, 6 countries)                        |
| **2. Integration and proof** | **Migrate the acquired book onto your own platform, rebrand, stand up the local function, produce the first repeatable artefacts**        | **Here, Aug 2026**                                                         |
| 3. Repeatable regional GTM   | Net-new wins under your own brand, reference customers, measurable RFP win rate                                                           | Not yet                                                                    |
| 4. Scale                     | Published regional targets, regional P&L narrative, named in investor commentary                                                          | Not yet                                                                    |

### The eight signals that place them at stage 2

1. **The rebrand is three months old.** "Key Travel by Trip.Biz" landed 20 May 2026. Integration is live, not finished.
2. **This is the first product role on the ground.** Kiran said so in July: "the first role of its kind." A function that needs founding is a stage 2 function.
3. **Sales and product are being hired in parallel.** Sr PM EMEA (req 6007), Sales Director EMEA (req 6352), Sales Executive EMEA (req 6945), all live at once. You hire both together when you are standing a motion up, not when you are scaling one.
4. **No published EMEA target exists.** No revenue, bookings or headcount number for EMEA corporate travel in any public source. Stage 4 businesses get numbers in the investor narrative. This one does not.
5. **Zero referenceability under the Trip.Biz brand in Europe.** No European awards, no G2 or Capterra listing, zero Trustpilot reviews, no published security, DPA or integration documentation. Every credential in the group's European B2B shelf belongs to Key Travel.
6. **The GM took the seat twelve months ago and sits on the target's board.** Jim Chung became GM EMEA and NA in July 2025 and joined the Key Travel board in August 2025. That is an integration mandate, not a growth mandate.
7. **The segment is small and just turned a corner.** Corporate travel is RMB 2.8bn of RMB 62.4bn FY2025 group revenue, about 4.5%. It grew 13% while the group grew 17%, so it lost share. Then Q1 2026 hit +20% against group +17%, above group for the first time in the run. A business that has only just started outgrowing its parent is not scaling yet.
8. **The Amsterdam EMEA hub was built at the same time as the acquisition**, giving a three-layer structure: Singapore global HQ, Amsterdam EMEA centre, six-country service network. New structure, not settled structure.

### ⚠️ The trap in this section, and it is the one that would puncture you

**Do not compare Key Travel's €187m to the RMB 2.8bn corporate travel segment.** BTN's European TMC rankings are measured in **sales / total transaction value**, not net revenue. Trip.com Group's RMB 2.8bn is **net revenue**. At a 3 to 5% take rate, €187m of TTV is roughly €6m to €9m of net revenue.

The correct read, and it strengthens the stage argument: **they did not buy revenue, they bought entry.** Six-country footprint, local service teams, a compliance and operating licence to trade, and a 46-year book of negotiated unpublished fares in humanitarian, faith and academic travel. That is a beachhead purchase, and beachhead purchases sit at stage 2 until the platform migration proves out.

## 2. What their very first priority is

**Answer: retain and migrate the Key Travel book onto the Trip.Biz platform with no churn, and make that migration produce the reference customers, RFP artefacts and implementation playbook that everything else in EMEA depends on.**

Revenue retention before revenue growth. Here is why that beats the obvious alternative.

| Candidate priority                       | Case for                                                                                                                                                                                 | Why it is not first                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Migrate and retain the acquired book** | It is the only defensible revenue in EMEA, the only proof the platform delivers in Europe, and the only source of European references. Jim is personally accountable via the board seat. | **This is first.**                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Win net-new EMEA enterprise business     | The JD names booking and revenue growth targets, and two sales roles are being hired now                                                                                                 | You cannot answer a European enterprise RFP without a security pack, a DPA position, integration specs and named references. Every one of those is a by-product of doing the migration properly. Growth is priority 2, and it is downstream. |
| Build the EMEA-native product            | Rail depth, e-invoicing, VAT, expense connectors and works council compliance are all real gaps                                                                                          | Too early and too expensive to guess at. The localisation backlog should be **discovered by the migration**, not invented ahead of it.                                                                                                       |
| Fix the AI to human handoff              | Their own EMEA BD lead named it as a live failure                                                                                                                                        | Real, but it is a workstream inside the migration, not a competing priority.                                                                                                                                                                 |

### The risk that makes it urgent

They bought a book whose loyalty was built on **human service** and are moving it onto a platform whose pitch is **AI automation**. Key Travel scores 4.6/5 across 861 reviews, and the praise is consistently for named human agents solving disruption by hand. Their own EMEA BD lead admits the AI to human handoff breaks. Key Travel's contact centre runs on a third-party stack (Vonage), so the handoff has to cross a system boundary Trip.Biz does not own.

If the migration degrades service, an unusually sticky book with unusually high switching costs churns anyway, and the entire EMEA thesis goes with it. That is the thing that can destroy the most value the fastest, which is what makes it priority one.

### The counter-read to hold in reserve

Jim owns the EMEA number, so he may well answer "growth" if asked directly. Do not argue. The two collapse into one anyway: the migration is the growth engine's prerequisite. If he says growth, agree and then ask what the migration has to deliver for the sales team to sell against.

## 3. What a Senior Product Manager is actually for, given that

Not a roadmap owner running discovery on new features. At stage 2, with that priority, the role is **the founding regional product lead who converts an inherited service business into a platform business, while brokering scarce engineering capacity out of an APAC headquarters.**

Five jobs, in the order they consume your week:

1. **Migration product owner.** Sequence which Key Travel client cohorts move when, define what must exist before each cohort moves, and hold the fallback. This is UAT, launch management and cohort risk, which is the exact shape of the BOC Pay four-region launch.
2. **Localisation backlog owner.** Every gap the migration exposes becomes a prioritised, HQ-negotiable roadmap item: rail depth, per-country e-invoicing, VAT and agent-versus-principal contracting, SSO and expense connectors, works council and GDPR constraints in DACH, per-carrier NDC.
3. **HQ capacity broker.** EMEA almost certainly queues against Shanghai or Singapore engineering. Where that capacity comes from is genuinely unknown from outside, so ask rather than assert. The role's real currency is winning sprint capacity for EMEA-specific work against APAC priorities. This is your Huawei job with the nouns changed.
4. **Presales product owner.** The JD names client presentations, solution demos, product marketing and global RFP responses. In EMEA corporate travel these are the deal. The artefacts are the deliverable: security pack, DPA position, integration specification, demo script, implementation and transition plan.
5. **Commercial translator.** Jim spent ten years in hotel contracting, chain negotiation and key accounts. He is not a product person. Every product argument has to be made in take rate, cost-to-serve, distribution cost per ticket and margin, not in discovery frameworks or scoring models.

**One line for the room:** *"From the outside it looks like the first year of this job is the Key Travel migration, and the product function gets built out of what that migration exposes."* Then stop and let him correct you.

### On #5, the part Korn Ferry cannot give you

KF #5 tells you the competency matters. It does not teach the domain. The specific things Jim can test in ten minutes:

- **TMC revenue lines**: transaction fees, management fees, SaaS subscription, supplier overrides, GDS segment incentives, virtual card interchange. The growth line is interchange.
- **The reframe that lands**: Navan's revenue is roughly 90% usage-based (commissions, per-trip fees, interchange) and only ~10% subscription, despite the SaaS branding. So "SaaS versus TMC" is a positioning story, not an economic one. Everyone still monetises the transaction.
- **Distribution economics**: Lufthansa charges €19 to €23 per GDS ticket against €8 via NDC from 1 Jan 2026. NDC is 31% of indirect sales in Europe against 18% in North America. Europe settles through IATA BSP, not ARC, so American statistics are a domain error here.
- **Travelfusion.** The group owns the largest LCC and NDC aggregator. `[[EMEA 差旅市場競品分析.pdf]]` treats it as Trip.Biz's structural moat in Europe. **The group ownership is checkable; the claim that Trip.Biz calls its API is that report's inference, not a sourced fact.** Ask it as a question, do not state it.

**Dive deeper:** `[[EMEA 差旅市場競品分析.pdf]]` §3.1 and §3.2 in this folder, plus [Platform Aeronaut on Navan's S-1](https://www.platformaeronaut.com/p/navan-s-1-and-tmc-industry-deep-dive) and [BTN's TMC fee models primer](https://www.businesstravelnews.com/SME/Primers/TMC-Fee-Models-Topline-Review). Korn Ferry has no competency for "decide what to build, for whom, and prove it moved a business outcome." The closest are Manages complexity (#8), Decision quality (#12) and Plans and aligns (#25), and none of them reach it. Named sources instead:
