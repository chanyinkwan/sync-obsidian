## Second Phase - Audit

### Audit Output

**1. Assumption Name: Shelf presence reveals operator intent.** That a brand appearing on 和記's shelf, at breadth, is evidence of 和記 *choosing* to back it. Ranging is a two-sided negotiated outcome; the blueprint reads a joint observable as unilateral operator behaviour.
- Found in: Track 1 A row 1 (「This IS 和記's device strategy, stated by their own merchandising」); Track 2 item 4; Track 3 Q3; Track 4 Page 1 headline claim.
- Classification: **Industry Convention presented as Fact.**
- Evidence Verification: The probe proves presence/absence of SKUs on six web shops on one day. It proves nothing about who initiated the listing, who funded it, or who carries inventory risk. European operator ranging is routinely bought: MDF, listing/slotting contribution, sell-in guarantees, price protection, buyback of unsold stock, co-funded subsidy. Honor's post-separation European playbook has been exactly that. 「和記 backs Honor」 and 「Honor pays to be on the shelf」 produce an **identical shelf**. Pure inferential leap: everything after the word 「扶持」.
- What Breaks (Risk): Page 1's headline claim inverts. A Huawei-internal deck that credits 和記 with a strategic preference, when the mechanism is actually vendor channel spend, teaches 朱總 the wrong lever — relationship-building instead of a channel-investment budget line.
- What Opens Up (Opportunity): Reframe Page 1 from 「whose strategy」 to **「what does a shelf slot cost」**. That is a price-of-entry analysis, far more actionable for Huawei, and partly sourceable from public reporting on Honor's channel investment.
- Inversion Test: Reversed, 和記 has no device preference at all — it tier-fills and monetises shelf space. Its real strategy is CAC/OPEX defence (already evidenced in the Dec-2025 workshop: CAC >10% of revenue, OPEX the universal pain, ARPU falling), and any vendor that lowers CAC gets ranged. 榮耀 is then not a favourite but the cheapest subsidy partner — displaceable by anyone cheaper, including us.

**2. Assumption Name: Shelf-versus-share divergence is actually computable.** That we will be able to place Honor's shelf allocation against Honor's market share and read the gap as deliberate operator behaviour.
- Found in: Track 2 item 4 (「This is the mechanism that would evidence 扶持榮耀 — and it is measurable rather than asserted」), dependent on Track 1 B bullet 3 and Track 1 D bullet 1.
- Classification: **Unverified Assumption** (Track 1 D itself admits the entitlement is unknown).
- Evidence Verification: Nothing in Phase 1 confirms a GfK / Counterpoint / Canalys entitlement, and the 「JP 報告」 named at 0:51 is unidentified. Free press summaries give region-level, brand-level, quarter-lagged share, not country × brand. Worse, the two sides are dimensionally incompatible: shelf is SKU-count, share is units sold. Comparing them requires a mix assumption that is itself unevidenced.
- What Breaks (Risk): This is the **only quantified mechanism in the entire blueprint** for the deck's central claim. Without it, Page 1 reverts to eyeballing a matrix — precisely the 「編一下」 that Ruling ② forbids.
- What Opens Up (Opportunity): Swap to a measure that needs no external data and is fully owned: tier span, SKU depth per brand per OpCo, and **generation currency** (does the brand's current flagship appear, or only last-gen). ## Experiment

> **Design rule:** each probe tests *feasibility of evidence* before it tests the claim. The failure mode we are pre-empting is not 「the claim is wrong」 — it is 「we built a page on a dataset that does not exist」.
