# SureCloud — Five Master Competency Stories

## Purpose

LinkedIn's practice interview indicates that the real screening is likely to use competency and behavioural questions rather than detailed product questions.

Use these five stories as a flexible evidence bank. Do not memorise 40 separate answers. Identify the competency being tested, select the closest story, change the opening and closing to match the question, and keep the factual middle of the story consistent.

## The 60-second behavioural structure

Use **STAR + Link**:

| Time | Section | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| 0-8 seconds | Situation | Give the context and challenge. |
| 8-15 seconds | Task | State what you personally needed to achieve. |
| 15-42 seconds | Action | Explain two or three actions you personally took. |
| 42-53 seconds | Result | Give the measurable outcome. |
| 53-60 seconds | Link | State what the experience proves for SureCloud. |

Keep the wording conversational. Learn the five-part logic and the numerical proof points rather than reading a script.

## Fast story selector

| If the question mentions... | Use this story | Core competency |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge, difficult problem, pressure, deadline, regulation | Story 1 | Problem-solving under pressure |
| Discovery, ambiguity, requirements, analysis, customer needs | Story 2 | Consultative discovery and scoping |
| Presentation, communication, objection, buy-in, adoption | Story 3 | Communication and stakeholder adoption |
| Customer focus, commercial value, qualification, sales, expansion | Story 4 | Commercial judgement and value selling |
| Disagreement, influence, resistance, change, no authority | Story 5 | Cross-functional influence and courage |

Throughout my career journey I consistently build the ability to uncover the real business problem, translate it for technical and commercial teams, prove the feasibility, and help business implement the solution. 

Priority:

I prioritise by weighing customer impact, urgency, and deadline flexibility.

At Apple, I had reserved time for major-account workshop preparation and a B2B discovery session when an unplanned business customer arrived on the sales floor. I judged that they needed an immediate response, but not a complete solution on the spot.

I time-boxed the conversation to 30 minutes, clarified their needs, captured the key context, and agreed a follow-up by email or phone. This gave the customer a responsive first interaction while allowing both sides to prepare for a more productive discussion.

I then returned to the protected time blocks, so neither the team session nor the workshop preparation slipped.

My approach is to assess impact and urgency, time-box interruptions, establish a clear next step, and protect critical commitments.
## Story 1 — Solving a regulatory challenge under pressure

### Primary competency question

**Walk me through a time when you faced a significant challenge and how you solved it.**

### 60-second framed answer

At Bank of China, I faced a significant challenge when an HKMA regulatory mandate required us to introduce Geo-IP fraud detection with impossible-travel logic within only 14 days. My responsibility was to turn the regulatory language into a solution that internal teams and a third-party vendor could actually deliver and validate. I broke the mandate into functional, data, testing, and approval requirements, clarified ownership and dependencies, and kept every decision tied to the regulator's required evidence rather than allowing the scope to expand. I also coordinated the technical teams and vendor so issues were resolved against the same acceptance criteria. We delivered within the deadline and met 100% of the regulatory mandates. It demonstrated that I can make a complex regulated problem executable, which is directly relevant to shaping GRC solutions for SureCloud customers.

### Three memory anchors

**HKMA — 14 days — 100% delivered**

### Flexible SureCloud closing

> That experience shows I can translate a complex obligation into clear requirements, coordinated action, and defensible evidence for a regulated customer.

## Story 2 — Turning discovery into validated solution scope

### Primary competency question

**Tell me about a time you uncovered a customer's real requirements and translated them into a solution.**

### 60-second framed answer

In my Huawei role, I support a Tier-1 UK mobile operator where the pursuit team needed a reliable technical scope before making commitments to the customer. I led three technical workshops and one structured requirements session with the customer's engineering stakeholders. I first mapped their objectives and current environment, then separated essential requirements from preferences. I tested the solution against European regulatory constraints across six customer subnets and documented the scope, risks, owners, and next steps for our eight-person team. As a result, the bid was built on requirements surfaced directly with the customer, and the team reduced the risk of promising something that had not been validated. At SureCloud, I would use the same discovery discipline so that every demonstration or PoC proves an agreed customer outcome rather than presenting generic features.

### Three memory anchors

**Three workshops — six subnets — validated scope**

### Flexible SureCloud closing

> It shows that I diagnose before prescribing and make both customer requirements and delivery risks visible before the solution is promised.

## Story 3 — Communicating a solution and securing adoption

### Primary competency question

**Tell me about a time you communicated a complex solution to different stakeholders and gained their buy-in.**


- Describe a measurable process improvement you delivered.

### 60-second framed answer

At Bank of China, teams spent approximately eight hours each week assembling reports across 20 systems. I helped replace that fragmented process with a single Grafana observability view, but the solution would create value only if the system owners and department leaders adopted it. In the demonstration, I led with their desired outcome — one live view instead of manual reporting — and then showed how the underlying data connected. I tailored the detail for more than 30 system leads and two department heads, and I answered their technical objections live rather than deferring them. The framework was adopted in that session and returned roughly 400 hours per year. At SureCloud, I would use the same outcome-first approach: start with the buyer's success metric, demonstrate the relevant workflow, and show only enough architecture to create confidence.

### Three memory anchors

**20 systems — 30-plus stakeholders — 400 hours returned**

### Flexible SureCloud closing

> It demonstrates that I can make a technical solution believable by connecting the workflow, stakeholder concern, and measurable business outcome.

## Story 4 — Discovering customer pain and creating commercial value

### Primary competency question

**Tell me about a time you identified an underlying customer need and turned it into commercial value.**

### 60-second framed answer

At Apple, a Hong Kong broadcaster visited without a formal enterprise opportunity. I recognised that the immediate request could be part of a wider business need, so I focused on understanding how the customer selected, purchased, and managed its devices. I built trust through hands-on technical support, created a tailored pricing portal, and qualified the operational and commercial requirements over several conversations. When the customer raised concerns about controlling a larger device fleet, I expanded the discussion from hardware purchasing to device management rather than treating it as a separate issue. The engagement became a £50,000 deal over three months, and this consultative approach contributed to £350,000 of B2B pipeline across my wider portfolio of more than 50 accounts. It shows the customer-led commercial behaviour I would bring to SureCloud discovery and value selling.

### Three memory anchors

**Casual visit — £50,000 deal — £350,000 pipeline**

### Flexible SureCloud closing

> It shows that I can move beyond the initial request, uncover the operational problem, and connect the right solution to measurable customer and commercial value.

## Story 5 — Influencing a cross-functional decision without authority

### Primary competency question

**Tell me about a time you challenged an existing decision and influenced people without formal authority.**


### 60-second framed answer

At Apple, headquarters planned to support a POS migration through one centralised training approach. The stores operated differently by location, so I believed the plan would create avoidable adoption and execution risk. I did not challenge the decision based on personal preference. I gathered evidence showing how store processes varied, translated those differences into the likely operational impact, and presented the case to the Shanghai development team. I also proposed a practical alternative: retain the common migration objective but deliver training in a store-specific way. The team reversed the original decision and moved to localised delivery. The experience taught me that effective influence means respecting the shared objective, making the hidden constraint visible, and offering a workable path forward. I would use the same approach when SureCloud customer evidence needs to influence Product or Engineering.

### Three memory anchors

**Central plan — store evidence — decision reversed**

### Flexible SureCloud closing

> It demonstrates that I can challenge constructively, use evidence rather than opinion, and turn field insight into an actionable cross-functional decision.

## How to adapt one story to different questions

Keep the factual middle of the story stable. Change only the first and final sentences.

### Example using Story 1

If asked about a **challenge**:

> A major challenge was translating a new regulatory mandate into a deliverable solution within 14 days.

If asked about **pressure**:

> The most demanding deadline I faced was a 14-day regulatory delivery at Bank of China.

If asked about **project coordination**:

> A strong example of cross-functional coordination was an HKMA-mandated fraud-detection change involving internal teams and a third-party vendor.

Use the same actions and result, then change the link to the competency in the question.

## Final rehearsal rule

For every answer, the listener should be able to repeat five things:

1. The challenge.
2. Your responsibility.
3. What you personally did.
4. The measurable result.
5. Why it matters to SureCloud.

If one of these is missing, the answer is not yet ready.
