# PAGE 2 — Where I See Potential Opportunities

Three reads, ranked by how much they change the roadmap. Each follows **Trend to Signal to Opportunity**, and each ends in the question I would want answered in week one.

## Opportunity 1 — Two motions, one roadmap

**Trend.** European business travel spend is growing on price, not on volume. Europe runs at roughly €360bn in 2025 rising to €389.9bn in 2026, up 8.2%, while global trip volume moves about 1.8bn to 1.84bn, near flat.<sup>1</sup> Growth therefore has to come from share of an existing trip count, which means capturing leakage and displacing incumbents. At the same time roughly 65% of global business travel spend is unmanaged and concentrated in SMEs, where only 15% to 25% run a managed programme against 85%+ of large enterprises.<sup>2</sup>

**Signal.** Trip.Biz has a high volume SME motion proven in APAC: the filed customer figure is 28,000 Chinese enterprises of which about 25,000 are SMEs.<sup>3</sup> What it acquired in EMEA is the opposite motion: Key Travel's book is universities, NGOs, humanitarian and faith organisations, managed and contracted, bought through RFPs, and retained on 46 years of negotiated unpublished fares.<sup>4</sup>

**Opportunity.** These two motions want different products. Self serve SME wants speed, low touch and a thin configuration surface. The managed non profit book wants certainty and flexibility: extended fare holds, free cancellation while on hold, no Saturday night stay rule, low change fees. The product decision is whether one platform serves both through configuration depth, or whether EMEA runs two. Getting that decision explicit is worth more in the first quarter than any feature.

> **The question:** you have a high volume SME motion proven in APAC and a managed, contracted non profit book in Key Travel. Those want different products. Does the EMEA roadmap serve both on one platform with configuration depth, or two?

## Opportunity 2 — Content and distribution is the unanswered question

**Trend.** Europe is ahead of North America on NDC: about 31% of indirect sales against 18%, settled through IATA BSP rather than ARC.<sup>5</sup> Lufthansa Group's Distribution Cost Charge from 1 January 2026 is €19 via Amadeus (raised from €18 on 5 May 2026), €22.50 via Sabre and €23 via Travelport, against €8 flat via NDC.<sup>6</sup> Air France KLM moved corporate NDC adoption from 10% to 27% during 2025 and targets 90% of indirect sales through NDC by 2027.<sup>7</sup> European rail is a first class corporate mode with its own distribution stack: Trainline Partner Solutions, Evolvi, SilverRail, Amadeus, standardising on OSDM.<sup>8</sup>

**Signal.** Trip.Biz markets NDC and LCC aggregation in the OBT, but no GDS or NDC partner is named publicly anywhere. Key Travel books rail, and the rebrand promises "the world's largest multi source inventory" including rail, but names no distribution partner either. Travelfusion sits inside Trip.com Group as an LCC and NDC aggregator, which from the outside looks like the obvious answer to European content.

**Opportunity.** This is a roadmap defining trade off, not a technical detail: own inventory gives margin and control but thin EMEA corporate content; GDS gives instant coverage at €19 to €23 a ticket; direct NDC gives €8 economics at the cost of per carrier engineering and slower time to market. The product mechanism that follows is the OBT ranking logic. If search results rank on total cost including distribution charge rather than on headline fare, the platform steers booking behaviour toward the cheapest channel by default, and the saving shows up in the client's programme cost without asking a traveller to change anything.

> **The question:** for EMEA air content, is the plan group owned inventory, a GDS relationship, direct NDC, Travelfusion, or Key Travel's existing arrangements? That choice sets the whole roadmap.

## Opportunity 3 — Referenceability is the constraint on every EMEA enterprise deal

**Trend.** EMEA corporate travel is not bought on review sites. It is bought through RFPs, GBTA and ITM peer networks, named client reference calls, security and DPA review, and incumbent displacement cycles. The JD puts global RFP responses and presales squarely in this role.

**Signal.** The group's European B2B credibility is real and it all sits under the Key Travel name: a 2022 Business Travel Awards Europe win, two 2025 BTN Europe finalist placements, four consecutive years in BTN's Europe Leading TMCs, plus ISO 9001, 14001 and 27001.<sup>9</sup> Under the Trip.Biz brand itself I could find no European award presence, no published SSO or SAML documentation, no published DPA or security pack, no Concur, Expensify or HRIS connector documentation, and no corporate buyer integration API. Meanwhile the competitive set publishes all of it openly, and travel plus expense convergence is the defining EMEA move of the last 18 months: Perk acquired Yokoy alongside its $200m Series E in January 2025 and dropped "Travel" from its name in November 2025, Navan ships native card issuance with Expense, Amex GBT relaunched Egencia in April 2026 with Concur Expense integration.<sup>10</sup>

**Opportunity.** Treat the credentials as a transfer problem, not a marketing problem. The migration produces the artefacts if it is run that way: a security pack, a DPA position, an integration specification, a demo script, an implementation and transition plan, and two named European reference accounts. That is an RFP kit, it is reusable across every subsequent bid, and it is a by product of doing the migration properly rather than a separate programme.

> **The question:** who can Trip.Biz put on a reference call today for a 5,000 traveller EMEA programme that is not a Key Travel non profit account?

## Held in reserve

- **ESG now sells on cost to serve, not on compliance fear.** Omnibus I, Directive (EU) 2026/470, in force 18 March 2026, raised the CSRD threshold to more than 1,000 employees **and** more than €450m turnover, removing roughly 80% of previously in scope companies, and listed SMEs came out of mandatory scope entirely.<sup>11</sup> Scope 3 Category 6 business travel survives for those still in scope. So the ESG module has to win on RFP score and cost, and the productisable gap is the one Trip.Biz's own GM for SEA named publicly: client carbon methodology mismatch forces manual mapping per account. A methodology mapping layer with an auditor ready export turns that admitted manual cost into a repeatable feature.
- **The AI indemnity, packaged as a procurement guarantee.** Trip.Biz covers the cost when Smart Approval gets a decision wrong. In an EMEA RFP that is worth more as a contractual SLA than as a product claim, because European procurement buys guarantees, not accuracy percentages. The precondition is the handoff: Trip.Biz's own EMEA BD lead has said that when AI cannot summarise the issue correctly, the human agent starts from scratch, and at Key Travel that handoff has to cross a third party contact centre stack.

# PAGE 3 — How I Can Contribute in This Role

## Where I fit the stage

From the outside, EMEA looks like stage two of four: the beachhead is bought, the rebrand is three months old, this is the first product role on the ground, and no public EMEA target exists yet. ## Three strengths, with proof units

### 1. I win headquarters decisions with commercial evidence, not escalation

*Huawei, Portfolio Solutions and Commercial Sales Specialist, London, May 2026 to present*

A China HQ roadmap built for global volume, against a Tier 1 European operator group buying across five country markets on its own commercial logic. I ran the full requirements cycle across three technical workshops plus a structured analysis session, turned customer input into documented solution scope, validated feasibility across six account subnets before proposal commitment, then argued a low value feature out of the device configuration through HQ's GTM roadmap and pricing reviews, on the evidence that it carried no weight in the customer's buying decision.

- **Outcome:** USD 2 per unit of product cost removed. I own bid responses and pricing analysis for the group, coordinating account managers, channel GTM operations and technical architects.
- **Why it transfers:** the JD's line about seamless collaboration with headquarters and APAC teams describes this exact motion. I did not ask HQ for a European product. I asked them to drop something Europe was paying for and nobody was buying.

### 2. I productise manual operations without breaking the service people bought

*Bank of China, Product Analyst, BOC Pay and e-Banking, Hong Kong, 2023 to 2024*

System health depended on inconsistent manual ping protocols and hand assembled weekly reports across 20 systems and 50+ teams. I built a centralised Grafana and Prometheus observability framework that replaced fragmented manual checks with real time signals in one dashboard, then drove adoption by demoing it live to 30+ system leads, the UX department head and the networking lead.

- **Outcome:** 50+ manual weekly reporting touchpoints retired, several hundred hours a year of manual reporting effort recovered, most technical objections closed in the first session.
- **Why it transfers:** the migration risk was never technical. It was that 50 teams each owned a spreadsheet, so I made the dashboard easier than the spreadsheet. Migrating a high touch account book onto an automated platform is the same problem with higher stakes.

*Apple, Operations Strategy Analyst, Hong Kong, 2021 to 2023*

Shanghai HQ mandated centralised, one size fits all training for the EasyPay POS migration. Store operations actually varied by location, so the rollout was set to break on the floor. I presented evidence of store level operational variance, moved the Shanghai development team onto store specific delivery, converted technical requirements into floor level playbooks, and prepared 10+ Floor Champions. When store leaders demanded a rollback mid migration, I showed them the original report flow still ran as a fallback and committed technical support through the transition.

- **Outcome:** full operational uptime through the migration, customer satisfaction held, adoption secured across all six flagship stores.
- **Why it transfers:** this is a service business being migrated onto a platform against local resistance, with a fallback held open. It is the Key Travel cohort migration in miniature.

### 3. I treat presales artefacts as product output

*Apple, Business Development, Corporate Solutions, Hong Kong, 2019 to 2021*

No systematic corporate pipeline existed in the region. I ran discovery, solution positioning and qualification end to end across a 50+ account portfolio, built diagnostic models that isolated 12 root causes of revenue variance, and on the Viu TV deal worked a walk in store visit into a qualified opportunity over three months, then cleared the device management objection by upselling MDM.

- **Outcome:** £350,000 in new B2B revenue, 30% lift in regional market penetration, £50K Viu TV close.
- **Why it transfers:** the custom business pricing portal was not a sales tool, it was the product artefact that made procurement say yes. That is what an RFP response kit is.

*Bank of China, regulatory delivery*

An HKMA account protection mandate with a hard 14 day deadline, on a product launching simultaneously across four global regions. I translated the regulation into a technical implementation plan, aligned internal teams and third party vendors, shipped Geo IP fraud detection with Impossible Travel logic, and designed a follow the sun 24 hour synchronised UAT schedule off a timezone gap analysis so no region sat idle.

- **Outcome:** 100% of mandates met inside 14 days, launch across four regions with no handover gaps, roughly two weeks of budget returned.
- **Why it transfers:** European localisation is the same shape of work. E invoicing, VAT treatment, works council constraints and SCA exemptions are compliance items that decide whether a deal is legal, and they are sequenced against a launch clock.

## First 30 / 60 / 90 days

The shape is diagnosis first, then a guiding policy, then a small number of coherent actions. I would expect the diagnosis to correct at least one of the reads on Page 2.

### Days 1 to 30 — Diagnose, and do not ship

1. **Map the operating spread.** Where EMEA engineering capacity actually comes from, who owns the EMEA roadmap, what the escalation path to Singapore and Shanghai is, and what the release cadence is. This determines what is buildable, so it comes first.
2. **Sit with the Key Travel service desk.** Ride along on humanitarian, faith and academic bookings until I can describe, in their words, what the fare desk does that the platform cannot yet do. Their moat is fare mechanics: extended holds, free cancellation while held, no Saturday night rule, change and baggage flexibility. Those are business rules waiting to become a policy engine.
3. **Pull the churn and cohort picture.** Which accounts renew when, which have already migrated, what the cost to serve per booking is by cohort, and where satisfaction is carried by named individuals rather than by the system.
4. **Read the last three EMEA RFPs won and lost**, and inventory what had to be written from scratch each time.

**Output at day 30:** a one page diagnosis with the migration cohort map and the top five localisation gaps, ranked by deal impact rather than by engineering cost.

### Days 31 to 60 — Sequence the migration and start the kit

1. **Publish the cohort migration plan.** Which client cohorts move when, what must exist before each cohort moves, what the rollback is, and who owns the call. Highest switching cost and highest touch accounts move last, after the platform has earned it.
2. **Fix the handoff before it is tested at scale.** Specify a context summarisation handoff that pushes a single incident card into the agent's workspace at the moment of transfer, so nobody is asked to explain their problem twice. Scope the integration to the existing contact centre stack rather than replacing it.
3. **Start the RFP response kit as a versioned product artefact**, not a document: security pack, DPA position, SSO and integration specification, SLA schedule, implementation and transition plan. Every migration cohort feeds it.
4. **Take one localisation item to HQ as a business case**, framed in RFP win rate and account retention rather than in feature terms, with a phased MVP so the first version costs HQ very little.

**Output at day 60:** a signed off cohort plan, a handoff specification in the backlog, and version one of the RFP kit.

### Days 61 to 90 — Prove it once, then make it repeatable

1. **Land the first cohort migration with retention and satisfaction intact**, measured as retained accounts and cost to serve per booking, not as features shipped.
2. **Convert two migrated accounts into named European references**, with permission secured in writing, because the sales hires now being made cannot sell an EMEA enterprise deal without them.
3. **Publish the EMEA localisation backlog** as a standing, HQ negotiable roadmap: rail depth, per country e invoicing, agent versus principal contracting and VAT consequences, expense and SSO connectors, DACH privacy constraints, per carrier NDC.
4. **Bring a position, not just a question, on content and distribution**, with the total cost per booking modelled across GDS, direct NDC and group inventory.

**Output at day 90:** one proven migration, two reference accounts, and a prioritised backlog that the sales motion and HQ can both plan against.

# APPENDIX B — Evidence discipline

## Numbers I will say

- Europe €360bn in 2025 to €389.9bn in 2026, up 8.2%; volume near flat at about 1.8bn trips. Growth is price, not volume.
- NDC at about 31% of indirect sales in Europe against 18% in North America; Europe settles through IATA BSP.
- Lufthansa DCC €19 Amadeus, €22.50 Sabre, €23 Travelport, against €8 via NDC.
- Corporate travel segment: RMB 2.8bn FY2025 of RMB 62.4bn group revenue, about 4.5%, growing 13% against group 17%, then Q1 2026 at +20% against group +17%.
- Key Travel: 15th largest TMC in the UK, 30th in Europe, about €187m European sales, six European markets.
- Omnibus I thresholds: more than 1,000 employees **and** more than €450m turnover.

## Numbers I will not say

- **Do not compare Key Travel's €187m to the RMB 2.8bn segment.** One is sales or transaction value, the other is net revenue. At a 3% to 5% take rate, €187m of TTV is roughly €6m to €9m of net revenue. They did not buy revenue, they bought entry.
- **Do not present Q1 2026's +20% alone.** A CFO literate interviewer knows the full year underperformed the group.
- **Do not say "you auto approve 99% of requests."** The 99% is a stated accuracy figure and the 98% is a stated approval rate. They are different metrics. Ask which is which.
- **Do not quote the $4m co-payment figure as a proof point.** Against 60m travellers claimed, it is pilot scale. The interesting question is what is blocking adoption.
- **Do not quote 1,000,000+ clients, 60,000,000+ travellers, 411,000 hours saved, or 87% AI resolution.** All unaudited company claims.
- **Do not claim Key Travel is IATA, ATOL or ABTA accredited.** Only ISO 9001, 14001 and 27001 are published.
- **Do not cite Key Travel's North America awards.** That division went to Globespan.
- **Do not say Perk moved its HQ to London.** Dual HQ Boston and London, Barcelona remains the largest site.
- **Do not raise the SAMR ruling.** Prepare it, let him raise it, and answer on forward controls and EU regulatory divergence rather than on blame.
- **Do not recite Key Travel's marketing copy back to a director of Key Travel.** The fare book read is offered as an outside view, then stopped.

## Framing to hold

- Lead commercial, hold product craft in reserve. Jim Chung is a supply side commercial leader, ten years of hotel contracting and key accounts. Product is a commercial instrument in this room. If a Head of Product joins the panel, the craft comes out then.
- Bilingual capability is the enabler underneath the pitch, never the pitch. Leading with "I am your bridge to HQ" is how a Senior PM gets rescoped into a liaison.
- Every read on Page 2 is stated as a read, then stopped, so he can correct it. The value is in the diagnosis, not in the recitation.
