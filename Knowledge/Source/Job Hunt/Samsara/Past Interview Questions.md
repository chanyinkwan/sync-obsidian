# Past Interview Questions — Samsara

Cleaned from raw Glassdoor reports. Candidate accounts, not official process. Two of the reports below describe a **sales rep** loop (MEDDICC, prospecting, Challenger), not a Sales Engineer loop, so treat those as adjacent context rather than a prep target.

---

## 1. The loop, as reported

Consistent across accounts:

1. **Recruiter screen** — 30 min.
2. **Hiring manager** — 45 min, mostly behavioural.
3. **Virtual onsite** — 3 peer interviews, 45 min each.
4. **Take-home assignment** + **30 min demo** of it to managers and directors, part role-play.
5. Some accounts add a **hardware installation exercise** (described as easy, takes minutes) and a **final director interview**.

Turnaround between rounds: within a few hours in the positive account.

Total time investment reported: up to ~9 hours across the process.

---

## 2. Questions asked

### Recruiter screen
- How have you handled difficult situations at work in the past?
- Why do you want to work as a sales engineer? / What drives you to become a sales engineer?

### Technical
- What is an API? Have you ever used them?
- What is your experience with APIs?
- What is your experience with hardware — multimeter, soldering?

### Behavioural
- Tell me about a time you had to deal with a difficult situation. What was the outcome?
- Talk about a time you had to explain a technical concept to a non-technical person.
- Give an example of when you took initiative to improve a process, or solved an issue quickly under pressure.
- Give an example of how you demonstrate ROI to a customer.
- A time you received feedback, and how you handled it.

### Sales-rep loop only (lower priority for ASE)
- STAR questions on prospecting, MEDDICC, and stakeholder management.
- Discovery call role-play with the manager: research the product from the website beforehand, structure the call, use TED questions, quantify impact.

---

## 3. What they are actually testing

From the most detailed account, the bar is:

1. You can work with APIs.
2. You can write basic scripts.
3. You can install their hardware.
4. You can explain a technical concept to a non-technical audience **and** tie it to business value.
5. You are coachable, feedback-oriented, and curious about the product. One director explicitly looked for the Challenger mindset.

---

## 4. Candidate experience — two opposite reports

**Positive.** Everyone friendly and responsive; questions answered properly; the process was framed as a two-way fit check, not just an evaluation. Take-home was interesting to build and present. Long but never stressful because it was well structured.

**Negative.** Reached the final stage, then was told to reapply in "a few months" because there was no active opening — described as talent pipelining. Feedback given was vague flaws "identified by every interviewer". Interviewers struggled to describe their own day-to-day, implying the role is largely sitting on calls providing technical support when needed. Reported low willingness to take candidate questions.

**Read:** the loop is long enough that it is worth confirming early that the req is live, and worth asking each interviewer what their last two weeks actually looked like.
