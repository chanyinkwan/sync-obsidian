# Block B — Market: AI-Native Bespoke Software for Physical Industries

## 1. Define the category in plain English

- The category is AI-native platforms that generate or run bespoke, high-stakes operational software for physical-world organisations (utilities, energy, infrastructure, construction, field services) — instead of the org buying an off-the-shelf ERP/EAM module or commissioning a custom-build from an IT consultancy. Cogna describes its own product as turning "AI across thousands of use cases legacy software never reached" (Cogna platform page, cogna.co, accessed 2026-08-21).
- Before this category existed, organisations in these sectors ran on three alternatives, all named directly by Cogna as what it displaces: legacy ERP/EAM systems (e.g., SAP, IBM Maximo-style asset/work management) with rigid, generic workflows; spreadsheets and manual, disconnected processes for anything the ERP didn't cover; and one-off custom builds commissioned from IT consultancies, which were slow and expensive (Cogna platform page, cogna.co, accessed 2026-08-21).
- Adjacent framing: Palantir's "Forward Deployed Engineer" model — embedding technical staff directly with the customer to build against real operational data rather than shipping generic software — is the delivery pattern this category is built on, and it is now being copied by OpenAI and Anthropic for enterprise AI deployment (MarkTechPost, 2026-05-20).
- Unanswered: no single, widely-agreed industry name for this category was found (analysts use "vertical AI," "vertical SaaS," or "AI-native enterprise software" interchangeably) — treat "AI-native precision software for physical industries" as a working label, not an established market term.

## 2. Why do organisations buy this at all?

- The business problem is not "we want AI" — it's that legacy ERP/EAM systems and generic software never reached large parts of operational reality (procurement edge cases, field service scheduling, gas escape management, etc.), leaving that work to run on spreadsheets, tribal knowledge, and manual coordination that doesn't scale and can't be audited (Cogna platform page, cogna.co, accessed 2026-08-21).
- Concretely, buyers cite: speed to value (Cogna case reference: "£11m saved in 4 weeks" on procurement optimisation, "£15m saved in first year" on an AI transformation, "83% reduction in manual work" for a field services client — cogna.co, accessed 2026-08-21); the ability to build on top of existing systems of record (SAP, Salesforce, SCADA, GIS) rather than ripping and replacing them, which matters to risk-averse operators of physical infrastructure (cogna.co, accessed 2026-08-21); and governance — a way to let non-technical operational teams get bespoke software without creating ungoverned "shadow IT," via built-in access controls and audit trails (cogna.co, accessed 2026-08-21).
- Underlying driver across the category: these are asset-heavy, safety- and compliance-critical operators (utilities, energy, infrastructure) where downtime, safety incidents, or missed regulatory reporting carry large financial and reputational cost, so the purchase is risk/cost avoidance and regulatory readiness, not novelty.

## 3. Which verticals adopt it hardest, and why

- Utilities (water, gas, electricity) and energy: named directly by Cogna as core verticals, with live customers Cadent Gas (procurement to gas-escape management) and Network Plus (cogna.co, accessed 2026-08-21). Palantir Foundry is separately in production use at PG&E (grid conditions, predictive maintenance, wildfire risk), an unnamed "energy supermajor" (production optimisation), Buckeye Partners (pipeline networks), and LG Energy Solution (utility-scale energy operations) (Palantir Foundry for Energy / Launch Consulting case study, accessed 2026-08-21).
- Infrastructure and field services: also named as core Cogna verticals (cogna.co, accessed 2026-08-21) — these are operations with distributed physical assets, mobile crews, and high coordination overhead, which is exactly the "legacy software never reached" gap the category targets.
- Construction is adopting more slowly but is a distinct, fast-growing adjacent vertical: the AI-in-construction market is estimated at $11.1B in 2025, forecast to reach $27.92B by 2031 (Mordor Intelligence, cited via search 2026-08-21) — growth here is tied to BIM/digital-twin mandates rather than the same ERP-replacement driver seen in utilities (see Q4).
- Why these verticals specifically: they share three traits that make bespoke AI software valuable — physical assets that must be tracked/maintained at scale, safety- or supply-critical operations where failure is expensive, and regulatory reporting obligations that generic software wasn't built to satisfy.

## 4. What regulation, standard or external mandate forces the purchase

- UK water — AMP8 (Ofwat's Asset Management Period 8, April 2025–March 2030): £104bn investment cycle, 77% more than AMP7, with digitalisation and data-driven asset management named as a central, non-optional priority of the period, not just compliance box-ticking (Causeway / multiple industry sources via search, accessed 2026-08-21). This is Cogna's own named vertical entry point (Cadent Gas is a gas — not water — utility, but the same AMP8-style regulatory pressure applies across UK utilities).
- UK/GB energy networks — Ofgem RIIO-3 (April 2026–March 2031): includes a formal "Digitalisation" licence condition requiring network companies to follow Data Best Practice Guidance and the NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework, with Final Determinations published 4 December 2025 (Ofgem, ofgem.gov.uk, accessed 2026-08-21). This is a direct regulatory mandate for the kind of governed, auditable digital systems this category sells into.
- Construction — BIM mandates: over 30 countries now mandate BIM on large public infrastructure programmes, including the UK's "Level 2 BIM" requirement on centrally-procured public projects (adoption reached ~73% of UK construction firms by 2020), EU public-procurement BIM/digital-twin mandates, US GSA "Single Source of Truth" BIM standards, and state-level mandates in Australia (Victoria, NSW) (multiple industry sources via search, accessed 2026-08-21). Net-zero targets are increasingly requiring digital-twin-ready BIM deliverables specifically, tying this mandate to decarbonisation policy as well as procurement policy.
- Unanswered: no single unifying regulation was found that spans all of utilities + energy + construction — each vertical has its own regulator and cycle (Ofwat/AMP8 for water, Ofgem/RIIO for GB energy networks, national/regional BIM mandates for construction); treat these as parallel, vertical-specific forcing functions rather than one category-wide mandate.
