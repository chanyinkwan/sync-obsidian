## 2. When would a customer choose Cogna over all of them?

Reasoned from Cogna's verified public positioning (not a direct quote list, but consistent across all sources):

- The customer has a workflow problem that is **specific to their operations** (not generic enough for off-the-shelf SaaS, but too narrow/local to justify a full SAP/Oracle module or years of consultancy customisation) — this is exactly the Network Plus framing: "unlike off-the-shelf software... custom solutions that fit our exact needs."
- The customer wants **speed measured in weeks, not the 6+ months and $1M+ that consultancies like Wipro/Capgemini typically charge for stakeholder mapping and build** (Peters' own stated contrast, The Innovator).
- The customer sits in a **physical/critical industry** (utilities, gas distribution, field services, infrastructure) where the tacit operational knowledge lives in people's heads, not in clean data — Cogna's stated differentiator is capturing that "domain context and decision logic" directly into the software (cogna.co positioning).
- The customer needs the output to **integrate with existing legacy systems** (SAP, Oracle, Databricks, Snowflake) rather than replace them — Cogna positions itself as sitting alongside, not ripping out, incumbent ERP.
- Security/compliance is a gating requirement but not a blocker: Cogna cites ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, Cyber Essentials, GDPR on its own site — relevant for utilities/critical infrastructure buyers with strict procurement bars.

## 3. When would a customer correctly choose someone else?

Reasoned, since no source states this explicitly (Cogna's marketing naturally doesn't publish "don't buy us if..."):

- **If the need is genuinely generic and high-volume** (standard HR, standard finance close, standard CRM) — off-the-shelf SaaS or a configured SAP/Oracle module will be cheaper per-seat at scale than a bespoke build, and won't carry single-vendor build risk.
- **If the customer needs a large, proven ecosystem of integrations, partners, and a deep bench of implementation talent already in-market** — Wipro/Capgemini/Accenture-tier SIs have decades of sector-specific delivery track record and can absorb far larger, multi-year, multi-country programmes than a ~108-person, 3-year-old startup (Tracxn headcount, June 2026) can currently staff.
- **If the organisation wants full internal control and IP ownership of a low-code citizen-developer capability** (i.e., wants its own business analysts building and maintaining apps long-term) — a platform like Mendix/OutSystems is the better fit; this is a reasoned category comparison, not one Cogna or press has made directly, so treat as inference only.
- **If vendor risk tolerance is low** — Cogna is Series A (~$19.8M raised total per Tracxn, June 2026), single-digit years old, with a customer base still concentrated in a handful of named logos (Cadent Gas, Network Plus, OCU Group, Ocado, Tropicana). A risk-averse enterprise procurement function may correctly prefer an incumbent with balance-sheet durability.

## 4. Most common competitive objection, and the honest answer

**Most likely objection, given Cogna's own site already pre-empts it: "AI doesn't understand our workflows / how do we know this will actually work for our specific, messy operational process?"** — Cogna's own website carries this exact objection-handling copy: "AI doesn't understand our workflows" is answered with "Cogna captures domain context and decision logic," and "Implementation takes too long" is answered with "proven 4-week to first-year delivery timelines." The fact that Cogna pre-answers these on its own homepage is itself the strongest evidence these are the real, live objections buyers raise (cogna.co, fetched 21 Aug 2026).

A closely related and very plausible objection not explicitly evidenced on Cogna's own materials but consistent with the "service-as-software" category and typical enterprise buyer behaviour: **"why not build this in-house with our own developers plus Copilot / an internal low-code team, instead of trusting a 3-year-old startup with a business-critical workflow?"** This is inferred from category context (Notion Capital's "service-as-software" framing explicitly targets the $25T services market being made viable by GenAI) rather than a documented quote — flagging as reasoned, not sourced.

**Honest answer (synthesised from Cogna's verified positioning, not a Cogna quote):**
- On "AI doesn't understand our workflows": Cogna's pitch is that its model is to extract the domain expert's tacit knowledge directly (via the "Blueprint" discovery process referenced on cogna.co) rather than assume the AI already knows the domain — this is a process/methodology claim, not a guarantee, and a buyer should ask for proof points (e.g., the Cadent Gas gas-escape-management case, OCU Group's 83% reduction in job-pack processing time) rather than take it on faith.
- On in-house build: the honest trade-off is speed and specialist focus (Cogna claims weeks-to-months delivery, cf. consultancies' 6+ months) versus long-term control and no vendor dependency. A buyer should weigh this exactly as they would any build-vs-buy decision — this is standard enterprise software judgement, not something unique to Cogna, and Cogna has not published anything that resolves the trade-off in its favour beyond speed/cost claims.
