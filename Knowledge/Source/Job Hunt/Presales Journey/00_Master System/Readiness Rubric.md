# Readiness Rubric

The gate is the only thing that decides readiness. Not hours spent, not how much research exists, not how you feel about it. Run by `/interview-prep gate`.

## Protocol

1. The skill draws 5 questions: 2 from the compulsory anchors, 3 from the stage's question bank, weighted toward the JD's keywords.
2. For each question you say the answer **out loud, with a timer running, materials closed**. Then you type what you actually said, in your own words. Dictation is fine.
3. The skill scores each answer on the four axes below.
4. Verdict and typed answers are appended to the tracker note's Gate Log with the date. That log is the proof, and it is what makes this externally scored rather than a feeling.

## The four axes

Each axis is worth 1 point. Maximum 4 per answer.

| Axis | Scores 1 when | Scores 0 when |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | STAR plus Link is present: situation, task, action, result, then a link back. | Any element is missing, or the answer is a list of duties rather than one event. |
| **Timing** | Self-reported delivery is 75 seconds or less. | Over 75 seconds, or you stopped the timer early because you lost the thread. |
| **Specificity** | At least one concrete number or one named artifact appears (a figure, a deadline, a document, a tool, a system). | Everything is directional: "significantly", "a lot of time", "several stakeholders". |
| **Company link** | The answer ends by tying the point to this company or this role. | The answer ends on the story itself and leaves the interviewer to infer the relevance. |

## Pass condition

**Pass = 4 of the 5 answers score at least 3 out of 4.**

Fail means the skill names the weak answers only. You redo those. You do not redo the whole prep.

## Worked example

Question drawn: **Tell me about a time you handled a serious objection from a customer or stakeholder.**

### Strong answer - 4/4

> "I have kept a deal alive after the buyer told me the product had failed their security review. This was a mid-size logistics customer, roughly a 120,000 pound annual contract, and the objection came from their head of infrastructure two weeks before signature. Rather than defending the product, I asked which specific control had failed and got the answer: no single sign-on for one admin module. I pulled the integration guide, ran a working SSO configuration in a sandbox with their identity provider, and sent a four-page validation note their security team could file as evidence. They re-scored the review and the contract signed on the original date. The reason I am telling you this one is that your buyers are regulated as well, so the technical win here depends on producing evidence security teams can file, not on winning the argument in the room."

| Axis | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | 1 | Situation, the objection, three specific actions, a result, then a link. All five present. |
| Timing | 1 | Self-reported 68 seconds. |
| Specificity | 1 | 120,000 pound contract, two weeks to signature, named artifact: the four-page validation note. |
| Company link | 1 | Ends on regulated buyers at this company and what that implies for the role. |

**Total 4/4.**

### Weak answer - 1/4

> "Yes, objections come up all the time in this kind of work. I think the main thing is to listen properly and not get defensive, because usually the objection is not really about what they are saying it is about. So I would normally take the concern away, speak to the technical team, and come back with a better answer. I have done that quite a few times and it generally works out well. Customers appreciate honesty more than a slick response, in my experience."

| Axis | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | 0 | No single event. This is a description of an approach, so there is no situation, action, or result. |
| Timing | 1 | Self-reported 41 seconds, within the limit. |
| Specificity | 0 | No number, no named artifact, no customer, no outcome. "Quite a few times" and "works out well" are directional. |
| Company link | 0 | Ends on a general opinion about customers. Nothing ties it to this company or role. |

**Total 1/4.** The fix is not to talk longer. It is to pick one real event and attach a number and an ending.
