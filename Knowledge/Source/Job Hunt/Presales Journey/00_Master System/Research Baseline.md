# Research Baseline

The minimum question set to research a company you are interested in, deep enough to hold your own through a second-round interview.

Reverse-engineered from the six-report SureCloud pack (Aug 2026), which is the most complete research run done so far. Those six reports map to six layers, and each layer answers a different interviewer.

**Rule: answer the ✅ questions in every block. The 🔍 questions are for when you reach a panel or a hiring manager who owns the number.**

| Block | Answers the question | Round 1 | Round 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Company | Who are they, really? | ✅ | 🔍 |
| B. Market | What game are they playing? | ✅ | 🔍 |
| C. Competitors | Who do they lose to, and why? | ✅ | 🔍 |
| D. Buyer | Who signs, and who blocks? | — | ✅ |
| E. Disruption | What is changing right now? | — | ✅ |
| F. The role | What does good look like in this seat? | ✅ | 🔍 |
| G. Your fit | Why you, and what do you ask them? | ✅ | ✅ |

**Time budget:** Blocks A, B, C, F and G at ✅ depth is about 90 minutes and gets you through a screen. Adding D, E and the 🔍 questions is a half day and gets you through a panel. Do not run the half day until a screen is actually booked.

---

## 0. Triage first (20 minutes, before anything else)

If these four do not produce a clear answer, stop. The role is not worth researching.

- [ ] What do they actually do, in one sentence a non-expert would understand?
- [ ] Who is the customer, and who writes the cheque?
- [ ] What problem do they solve, and what happens to the customer if they do nothing?
- [ ] Why does this role help my positioning, not just my income?

---

## A. The Company

✅ **Must answer**
- [ ] Founding story, ownership and capital position. Who owns them: founders, VC, private equity, public? What round or exit was last, and when?
- [ ] What do they sell, module by module, and which product line actually earns the revenue?
- [ ] Who are the named customers, and which one is the reference case they lead with?
- [ ] What is the stated mission, and what do employee reviews say about whether it holds?
- [ ] What is their headcount, growth rate and hiring pattern in this function over the last year?

🔍 **Go deeper**
- [ ] Revenue or ARR estimate, valuation, and burn or profitability signal.
- [ ] Platform architecture and the one technical decision they claim as a differentiator.
- [ ] Their AI strategy, if any: is it a product, a feature, or a press release?
- [ ] Partnerships and channel: who resells or integrates them?
- [ ] Strategic risk matrix. What would kill this company in three years?

## B. The Market

✅ **Must answer**
- [ ] Define the category in plain English. What was the world like before this category existed?
- [ ] Why do organisations buy this at all? Name the business problem, not the feature.
- [ ] Which verticals adopt it hardest, and why those?
- [ ] What regulation, standard or external mandate forces the purchase?
- [ ] Market size and growth rate, with a source and a date.

🔍 **Go deeper**
- [ ] Sub-market breakdown: which segment is growing and which is flat?
- [ ] Regional share. Where is this company strong and where is it absent?
- [ ] The three trends reshaping the category right now.
- [ ] Five-year horizon. What does the category look like when the current trend finishes playing out?

## C. The Competitors

✅ **Must answer**
- [ ] Name the top 5 competitors and split them into legacy incumbents versus fast-moving challengers.
- [ ] When would a customer choose this company over all of them? Be specific.
- [ ] When would a customer correctly choose someone else?
- [ ] What is the single most common competitive objection, and what is the honest answer?
- [ ] Where do analysts place them: Gartner, Forrester, G2, peer reviews?

🔍 **Go deeper**
- [ ] One-paragraph deep dive per competitor: strength, weakness, price posture.
- [ ] The evaluation matrix a buyer would actually build, and where this company scores badly.
- [ ] The competitor whose existence is the biggest threat to this company's next three years.

## D. The Buyer

✅ **Must answer** (second round)
- [ ] Map the buying committee. Who is the economic buyer, the champion, the technical gatekeeper, the blocker?
- [ ] What does each persona care about, in their own words, and what does each fear?
- [ ] Walk the buying lifecycle end to end: awareness, evaluation, selection, proof of value, implementation, adoption, expansion.
- [ ] What are the top win drivers and the top loss drivers on deals in this category?
- [ ] What internal politics kill deals here, independent of product quality?

🔍 **Go deeper**
- [ ] Typical deal size, sales cycle length and procurement path.
- [ ] What a proof of concept looks like, and how it gets governed and scoped.
- [ ] Real implementation case studies: what went wrong, and how long it took.

## E. The Disruption

✅ **Must answer** (second round)
- [ ] What technology shift is changing this category right now, and is it real or vendor noise?
- [ ] Disambiguate the buzzwords. What do the terms actually mean in this domain, and where does this company genuinely sit?
- [ ] How is the shift changing buying behaviour, not just the product?
- [ ] What new risk does the shift introduce for the customer, and what is the trust or governance answer?
- [ ] What does each major vendor claim on this front, and who is furthest along?

🔍 **Go deeper**
- [ ] How would you measure ROI on the new capability? Build the model.
- [ ] Three-to-five-year prediction, with your reasoning stated so you can defend it.

## F. The Role

✅ **Must answer**
- [ ] What does this function actually do day to day at this company, at this stage and size?
- [ ] What qualification and discovery method does the function use here? MEDDPICC, SPIN, or something in-house?
- [ ] What are the KPIs for this seat? What number would you be measured on at 6 months?
- [ ] Who does the seat sit between, and what does each side need from it?
- [ ] What does the first 90 days look like: 30 days learning, 60 days co-piloting, 90 days owning?

🔍 **Go deeper**
- [ ] The technical validation motion: demo, proof of concept, architecture review, or all three?
- [ ] How value engineering and the business case get built here, and by whom.
- [ ] The commercial boundary. Does this seat touch pricing and negotiation, or stop before it?

## G. Your Fit and Your Questions

✅ **Must answer, every time**
- [ ] Which 3 of your real, evidenced experiences map to their top 3 buying criteria? Name the bullet, not the vibe.
- [ ] What is your honest gap against this role, and what is the one-sentence bridge? No apology.
- [ ] What are the 4 or 5 interview questions they are most likely to ask, given everything above? Draft the answers.
- [ ] What are 5 questions **you** ask them that only someone who did this research could ask?
- [ ] What would make you turn this job down? Decide before the offer, not during it.

## What "enough" looks like

Stop researching when you can do these three things without notes:

1. **Explain their business to a smart outsider in 60 seconds**, including who pays and why.
2. **Argue the other side.** Name a customer profile that should buy a competitor instead, and say why. If you cannot do this, you have read marketing, not research.
3. **Ask one question that makes the interviewer pause.** Usually it sits at the seam between two blocks: a market trend against their competitive position, or a buyer's fear against their product roadmap.

## Notes on running this

- **Do not run all six blocks before a screen is booked.** The SureCloud pack was six deep reports; that depth is a second-round investment, not a triage cost. Blocks A, B, C, F, G first.
- **Every claim needs a source and a date.** Analyst pages, G2 and PitchBook figures go stale, and an interviewer who owns the number will know the current one.
- **Reuse across competitors.** Blocks B, D and E are category-level, not company-level. Research a category once and it serves every company in it. The Samsara pack largely transfers to [[Geotab - Solutions Engineer]]; only Blocks A and C need rebuilding.
- **Output lives in `Knowledge/Source/Job Hunt/Presales Journey/Companies/<Company — Role>/Research/`**, alongside the role-specific materials. Link the opportunity back to [[Application Pipeline]].
