## Part II — The Full Consult

### 0. First, a criticism of the brief itself

You asked me to criticize the approach if I disagree. I do, on one point, and it's the most important one:

**This is not an exploration. You already made this decision and you're asking for permission to believe it.**

Your own CLAUDE.md — written 2026-06-12, three weeks ago — lists your target roles: *Deal Desk Manager, Large Customer Sales Analyst, AI Solutions Engineer*. Two of those three are the Presales/Solutions path. You then built a "terminology directory" to translate your daily work into solutions-engineering language, and a weekly SCQA canvas to practice solutions-engineering communication. Your system has been quietly voting for Path 1 for weeks. The "three paths" framing is a ritual of fairness toward two options you've already outgrown.

The second flaw: the three paths aren't three paths. Presales and post-sales are two ends of the same solutions lifecycle, and "AI PM" as you describe it ("shipping end to end") is not a role description — it's a *feeling* you want ("I made a thing and it exists now"). So the real question was never "which of three jobs" — it's **"which job title lets me get paid for building systems, instead of building systems to survive a job that doesn't need me to think?"** That question has one answer, and we'll get there.

### 1. Psychological diagnosis: the friction, unpacked

#### 1.1 The core tension is real, and it's not laziness

You have an MSc in Business Analytics (0.2 marks from distinction — more on that number later), you think in frameworks, and you spend your days doing sample-management logistics and admin coordination. That mismatch produces a specific kind of fatigue: not overwork, but **under-utilization dressed as busyness**. You end days tired without the compensating feeling of having *built* anything. This is legitimate friction. You're not imagining it, and no amount of "gratitude for having a job at Huawei" fixes it.

#### 1.2 The shadow: your greatest strength is currently running as an avoidance program

Look at the pattern across your entire history:

- Apple retail → unprompted, you build Excel models and insight systems for the store
- BOC Pay → you gravitate toward the process/communication architecture of shipping
- Huawei → within months you've built: a Claude-automated vault, a CV generator with a render pipeline, a meeting-transcript-tidying skill, sync scripts, a "Dual-Track Frictionless Growth Loop," a 4-question daily canvas, and a standardized terminology directory

Every time you're dropped into an operational role, you respond by **building the system the role should have had**. That is genuinely your signature strength — it's the most consistent fact in nine years of otherwise fragmented history.

But here is the hard part: at Huawei, the system-building has metastasized into something else. A 4-question daily reflection canvas, McKinsey SCQA weekly rituals, 20% protected "strategic blank space," a terminology translation layer — this is **a cathedral of process built around a job that doesn't need one**. When the work itself can't feed you, you build increasingly elaborate systems *about* the work, because building is the only part of the day where you feel like yourself. The Career Hub is simultaneously your best proof-of-capability and your most sophisticated procrastination engine. It lets you feel strategic *about* your career as a substitute for making the one strategic move your career actually requires.

The prescription is not "stop building systems." It's: **get a job where the system IS the deliverable**, so the energy stops leaking into meta-work.

#### 1.3 The self-discounting tic

Notice how you presented your own evidence: "Building excel sheets/models for Apple **(But only for one store)**." You pre-shrink your accomplishments before anyone else can. Same with "merit (0.2 marks from distinction)" — you're carrying that 0.2 like a debt. A hiring manager never sees the 0.2; *you* re-litigate it. This tic matters commercially: presales is a confidence-transfer profession. Interviewers will buy your framing of yourself at the price you set. Right now you're pricing yourself at "admin who dabbles." The Apple story, told properly, is: *identified an analytics gap no one asked me to fix, built the tooling, and it changed how the store made decisions.* Scale is a detail; initiative-plus-artifact is the headline.

#### 1.4 The résumé arithmetic a hiring manager actually does

Ruthless section. Your history through top-tier tech/banking eyes:

| Chunk | Their read |
|---|---|
| 1y data intern (cleaning) | Junior, discounted |
| 2y media assistant, HKJC | Off-track, discounted |
| 3y part-time specialist | Part-time = discounted heavily |

Nine calendar years compress to roughly **two years of credited, analyst-level-or-above experience**. That's the brutal number. It means: (a) any path requiring you to be hired *for seniority* is closed right now; (b) any path that lets you be hired for *demonstrated system-building plus enterprise context* is open; (c) the narrative connecting these chunks has to be built by you, deliberately, because left raw it reads as drift. The good news: the connecting thread genuinely exists (§4).

### 2. Real vs. Ideal: the three paths under interrogation

#### Path A — Presales / Solutions Engineering
**Ideal version:** building demo systems, mapping customer problems to architecture, optimizing the deal workflow.
**Real version:** …actually pretty close to the ideal. Discovery calls, demo construction, RFP responses, competitive positioning, internal deal navigation. It is the only role of your three where "build the system, translate it to a business audience" is the *job description* rather than your private coping mechanism.
**What you're missing:** deep product-specific technical depth. Fixable — SE orgs hire for translation ability and domain context, then train product depth. Your Huawei enterprise/telecom exposure plus fintech product year plus analytics MSc is a legitimate entry profile for SE/Solutions Consultant at AI SaaS or cloud infra — especially at the many companies selling *into* telecom and enterprise.

#### Path B — AI Product Manager ("extremely AI or Cloud")
Here's where I have to break something, with precision.

You already ran the PM experiment. BOC Pay. Your own words describing it: *"closer to project management but **more internal communication**."* That sentence is the entire PM job at scale. At an AI-first or cloud company, PM is 70% stakeholder alignment, roadmap defense, and internal communication — the exact texture of work you found draining enough to describe dismissively. The "shipping high" you're chasing lives in maybe 10% of a PM's calendar. **You are nostalgic for the artifact, not the role.**

And the market reality: "AI PM at an extremely AI company" in 2026 is one of the most oversubscribed roles on earth. You'd be competing against people with shipped AI products, ML backgrounds, or FAANG PM pedigrees — with your two credited years and zero AI products on the résumé. The Career Hub is a lovely personal AI project; it is not a shipped product with users, and every AI PM interviewer will make that distinction in the first five minutes. This path is a **career reset to APM-level competition at maximum-difficulty companies**. I won't pretend otherwise.

(The honest footnote: PM isn't closed *forever*. Solutions Engineer → Product is one of the most-traveled internal bridges in SaaS, because SEs arrive with field evidence PMs desperately need. If the PM itch survives 2-3 years of SE work, you cross the bridge with leverage instead of resetting without it.)

#### Path C — Post-sales / Onboarding / Customer Success
**Leverage is fine** — B2B support, customer-facing retail years, communication skill. You'd get hired.
**But run the tape forward:** onboarding at most SaaS companies is executing a playbook someone else wrote. Within six months you'd have automated your own workflow (you always do), and then you'd be… an under-utilized systems thinker doing repeatable operational work, journaling about strategic blank space. **Path C is the Huawei situation with a different logo.** You'd be choosing your current pain at a new address. The only version worth considering is implementation/onboarding *engineering* at a complex-deployment product — and that's just presales' sibling, which strengthens the case for Path A.

### 4. The Lock-In

#### Target role for the next phase (2026–2029):
**Solutions Engineer / Presales Solutions Consultant at an AI SaaS or cloud-infrastructure company** — ideally one selling into enterprise/telecom, where your Huawei context converts from "admin job" into "domain moat". This is also exactly the "AI Solutions Engineer" your own CLAUDE.md already committed to; consider this the external validation you were structuring this exercise to obtain. Deal Desk remains a natural adjacent/backup lane (same commercial-systems muscle, more internal-facing).

#### The translational architecture — your story, made non-accidental:

> *"I've spent my career on every side of the commercial table — retail floor, B2B support, fintech product team, enterprise vendor — and in every seat I did the same thing: found the gap between what the business needed and what the systems delivered, and built the bridge. Excel decision-models in retail. Product delivery and cross-functional communication at BOC Pay. Competitive analysis, account strategy, and workflow automation at Huawei. Solutions engineering is that same motion, done deliberately, for customers."*

Retail → Support → Product → Enterprise sales isn't drift anymore. It's **360° of the customer lifecycle, converging on the role that sits at the center of it**. That's the frame. Every interview answer hangs off it.

#### The immediate next step (start this week, 14-day scope):

**Turn the Career Hub into your demo — because a Solutions Engineer's job interview is literally "demo a system and explain your architecture choices to a business audience."** You already possess the exact artifact the role tests for; it's just currently invisible.

1. **Days 1–4:** Write a one-page case study of the Career Hub as a solutions document: business problem → requirements → architecture (Obsidian + Claude automation + CV render pipeline + sync scripts) → measurable outcome (hours saved, artifacts produced). Write it exactly like a customer-facing solution brief. Record a 5-minute screen demo.
2. **Days 5–7:** Reposition the LinkedIn headline and About section from "sales support" to solutions language — you already built the terminology directory for this; deploy it. Headline shape: *"Solutions & Presales | Enterprise Telecom + Fintech Product | I build the systems that close the gap between customer problems and product."*
3. **Days 8–14:** Mint one *real* presales artifact at Huawei you can own by name. You already have two sitting in your Tasks folder: the **FWA Roadmap section** and the **Account Gap & Opportunity Analysis**. Finish one to customer-presentable quality and ask your manager (in your existing SCQA slot — the ritual finally earns its keep) to let you present it in an account context. That's the line on the résumé that says *presales*, not *admin*.

Then apply to Solutions Engineer / SE Associate / Solutions Consultant roles at AI SaaS and cloud companies — 10 applications in the following 30 days, every one using the case study as the attached work sample.

#### What to stop doing:
- **Stop upgrading the vault.** The meta-system is finished. Every additional hour of process architecture is an hour stolen from the three deliverables above. The next system you build should be one a hiring manager watches you demo.
- **Stop the "only for one store" tic.** In writing, first. The case study contains zero self-discounting qualifiers.

### 5. The one-paragraph verdict

You are not confused about what you want; you're afraid the modest-sounding option is beneath the person the MSc was supposed to prove you are. So "AI PM at an extremely AI company" persists as the prestige fantasy while your actual behavior — every unprompted system you've ever built — points at solutions engineering, and has for years. Lock in Presales/Solutions. It's not the consolation prize; it's the only role on your list where the thing you compulsively do when nobody's watching is the thing they pay you for.

##### 路徑 B — AI 產品經理（AI Product Manager，強調「硬核 AI 或雲端」）

這裡我必須精準地擊碎一些幻想。

你已經做過 PM 的實驗了。在中銀香港 BOC Pay。你用自己的話形容它：「更接近項目管理，但主要是內部溝通。」**這句話就是 PM 大規模運作時的完整工作內容。** 在一家 AI 優先或雲端公司中，PM 70% 的時間都在做利益相關者協調、捍衛產品路線圖和內部溝通 — 這恰恰是你覺得非常消耗能量、以至於用輕蔑語氣描述的工作本質。你所追求的「交付產品時的興奮感」大概只佔 PM 日曆表中的 10%。你現在對 PM 的嚮往只是流於對「產出物（artifact）」的浪漫懷舊，而不是真正鍾意那個角色。

而且市場的現實是：2026 年的「硬核 AI 公司的 AI PM」是全球競爭最慘烈的職位之一。你將要與那些擁有已交付 AI 產品經驗、機器學習（ML）背景、或具備 FAANG PM 履歷的人競爭 — 而你的履歷上只有兩年被認可的經驗，且 AI 產品交付經驗為零。Career Hub 是一個很棒的個人 AI 專案；但它不是一個擁有真實用戶、已上線交付的產品，每位面試 AI PM 的面試官都會在前五分鐘內挑出這個區別。這條路徑意味著你要在難度最高的公司裡，重新回到 APM（助理產品經理）級別去內卷。我不會對此粉飾太平。

_(誠實的補充：PM 這條路並非永遠封閉。在 SaaS 行業中，Solutions Engineer → Product 是一條最常被走通的內部晉升橋樑，因為 SE 帶著 PM 迫切需要的市場一線實戰證據。如果 PM 的想法在做過 2-3 年 SE 工作後依然強烈，你可以帶著一線的槓桿優勢跨過這座橋，而不是現在毫無籌碼地砍掉重練。)_

##### 路徑 C — 售後 / 客戶導引 / 客戶成功（Post-sales / Onboarding / Customer Success）

你的槓桿優勢完全沒問題 — B2B 客戶支援、面對客戶的零售經驗、溝通技巧。你肯定能被錄取。

但讓我們把畫面往後快進：在大多數 SaaS 公司中，客戶導引（Onboarding）只是執行別人寫好的劇本。不到六個月，你就會把自己的工作流程自動化（你總是會這樣做），然後你就會變成……一個大材小用的系統思考者，重複做著機械式的營運工作，並在筆記本裡撰寫關於「戰略留白空間」的日誌。路徑 C 只是換了新公司 Logo 的「華為現狀」。你只是在一個新地址選擇了你現在的痛苦。唯一值得考慮的版本是複雜部署產品下的實施/導引工程師（Implementation/Onboarding Engineer） — 但那只是售前的親兄弟，這反而更強化了支持路徑 A 的論點。

#### 3. 職涯路徑匹配矩陣

評分 1-5 分。**槓桿契合度（Leverage Fit）** = 能使用你現有的背景，而不需要像初階入行那樣砍掉重練。**動能回報（Energy Return）** = 能滿足你建構/優化的渴望，並逃避真實角色中的行政停滯（以真實角色為準，而非想像中的理想狀態）。

|**路徑**|**槓桿契合度**|**動能回報**|**總分**|**一句話裁決**|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**售前 / 解決方案工程**|**4**|**5**|**9**|**在這個職位上，你的應對機制直接變成了你的交付物。**|
|AI 產品經理 (AI/雲端原生)|2|3|5|在市場競爭最慘烈的門口砍掉重練，去追求一個該角色極少能提供的感覺。|
|售後 / 客戶導引 / CS|4|2|6|很容易拿到 offer，而這正是陷阱所在 — 它會完美重現你正在逃離的停滯感。|

_關於兩個可能會引發爭議的分數的註解：_

- _AI PM 動能回報 = 3，而不是 5：這是針對真實角色（以內部溝通為主）進行的評分，你已經有直接的證據證明自己不喜歡這種工作，而不是針對那個幾乎不存在的理想角色（純粹的產品交付）。_
    
- _售後槓桿契合度 = 4：這確實很容易面試進去。槓桿契合度衡量的是准入門檻成本，而不是退出價值。它的退出價值對你而言非常糟糕。_
    

#### 4. 最終決定（The Lock-In）

**下一階段（2026-2029）的目標崗位：**

**AI SaaS 或雲端基礎設施公司的 Solutions Engineer / 售前解決方案顧問** — 最理想的是賣給企業/電信商的公司，這樣你的華為背景就能從「行政工作」轉化為你的「領域護城河」。這也正正就是你自己的 `CLAUDE.md` 早就承諾過的「AI Solutions Engineer」；把這看作是你在安排這次練習時，所渴望獲得的外部驗證吧。Deal Desk 依然是一個自然的鄰近/後備賽道（使用同樣的商業系統肌肉，但更偏向內部）。

**專有名詞轉譯架構 — 讓你的故事看起來不再是偶然：**

> 「我的職涯橫跨了商業辦公桌的每一側 — 零售一線、B2B 客戶支援、金融科技產品團隊、大型企業供應商 — 而在每一個座位上，我都做了同一件事：找出商業需求與系統交付之間的鴻溝，並搭建起橋樑。零售業中的 Excel 決策模型；中銀香港 BOC Pay 的產品交付和跨部門溝通；以及華為的競爭分析、賬戶策略和工作流自動化。解決方案工程（Solutions engineering）正是同一個動作，只是更有意識地為客戶去執行。」

零售 → 客戶支援 → 產品 → 企業銷售，這不再是四處飄泊。這是 360 度完整的客戶生命週期，最終匯聚在位於其核心的角色上。這就是包裝框架。每一個面試回答都應該圍繞著它展開。

**立刻執行的下一步（本週開始，為期 14 天）：**

將 Career Hub 轉化為你的 Demo — 因為解決方案工程師的面試，本質上就是「演示一個系統，並向商業受眾解釋你的架構選擇」。你已經擁有了該角色測試所需的完全相同的產出物；只是它目前處於隱形狀態。

- **第 1-4 天：** 將 Career Hub 寫成一份 1 頁紙的個案研究，包裝成了解決方案文件：商業問題 → 需求 → 架構（Obsidian + Claude 自動化 + 履歷渲染管線 + 同步腳本） → 可衡量的結果（節省的時間、產出的成品）。完全按照面向客戶的解決方案簡報（Solution Brief）來寫。並錄製一段 5 分鐘的螢幕 Demo。
    
- **第 5-7 天：** 將 LinkedIn 的 Title 和 About 欄位從「銷售支援（sales support）」重譜為解決方案語言 — 你已經為此建構了專有名詞字典；直接部署它。Title 格式：`Solutions & Presales | Enterprise Telecom + Fintech Product | I build the systems that close the gap between customer problems and product.`
    
- **第 8-14 天：** 在華為親手打造一個你可以署名的真實售前產出物。你的 Tasks 資料夾裡已經放了兩個現成的東西：FWA Roadmap 部分，以及 Account Gap & Opportunity Analysis（賬戶差距與機會分析）。將其中一個完成至可以向客戶演示的級別，並要求你的主管（在你現有的 SCQA 時間段內 — 這個儀式終於發揮了它的價值）讓你把它應用在真實的賬戶場景中。這才是履歷上寫著「售前（presales）」而不是「行政（admin）」的那行字。
    

然後開始向 AI SaaS 和雲端公司的 Solutions Engineer / SE Associate / Solutions Consultant 崗位投遞簡歷 — 在接下來的 30 天內投遞 10 份申請，每一次都要將這份個案研究作為隨附的工作範本。

**必須停止做的事：**

- **停止再去升級你的知識庫（vault）。** 這個後台系統已經完工了。你在流程架構上多花一個小時，就是從上述三個具體交付物中偷走了一個小時。你建構的下一個系統，應該是能讓招聘經理坐在對面看著你演示（Demo）的系統。
    
- **停止「只針對一家門市」這種自我打折的壞習慣。** 首先在書面表達上改掉它。個案研究中不要包含任何自我貶低的限定詞。
    

#### 5. 一段話的最終裁決

你並不是對自己想要什麼感到困惑；你只是害怕這個聽起來低調的選項，配不上你的碩士學位本該去證明的那個高度。所以「硬核 AI 公司的 AI PM」作為一個滿足虛榮心的幻想一直揮之不去，而你真實的行為模式 — 你在沒有要求的情況下建構的每一個系統 — 好幾年來都精準地指向了解決方案工程。**鎖定售前/解決方案吧。** 這不是退而求次的安慰獎；這是你清單上唯一一個，能把你「在沒人看著你時強迫症般去做的事」直接變成「他們付錢請你去做的事」的職位。
